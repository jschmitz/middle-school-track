Practice 1 — Welcome & First Run
Practice 1 — Wednesday, August 19

Welcome & First Run

First practice of the fall cross country season. The goals today are simple: meet each other, learn the routine, get moving together, and have fun. Athletes finish with a game; parents stay for a short end-of-practice meeting with the coaches.

1 — Welcome & Introductions 8 min

Set the tone before anyone runs a step. This few minutes shapes how athletes feel about the whole season.

  • Gather in a circle — athletes and coaches together. Keep parents nearby but separate for now; they’ll have their own time at the end.
  • Coaches introduce themselves. Name, connection to the school, and one sentence on why you coach. Keep it short and genuine. Example: “I’m Coach Jake. I have a kid on this team, I love running, and I can’t wait to see what you all do this fall.”
  • Athlete introductions — go around the circle: Name and one thing — “what was something fun you did this summer?” Nobody skips, nobody is put on the spot for more than that.
  • Season overview in two sentences: “We practice Wednesdays and Fridays, 5 to 6. The season runs through October. Today we’re going to run together, do some drills, play a game, and have a great time.”
  • The one team rule: “We don’t compete against each other in practice. We push each other. There’s a big difference.”

2 — Easy Warm-Up Jog 5 min

First group run of the season. Keep everyone together — no racing the warm-up.

  • Coach leads at a comfortable conversational pace. Athletes follow as a group around the perimeter of the baseball field.
  • This is Z1 effort — full sentences, no effort at all. If anyone is breathing hard, slow down.
  • Use this jog to keep the intro energy going: “Hey, find someone you don’t know yet and introduce yourself while we jog.”
  • Watch who is already comfortable and who is working. Note it mentally — this is your first read on tiers before anything formal.

3 — Dynamic Warm-Up 8 min

Teach this routine today. We run the same sequence every practice so athletes can eventually lead it themselves.

  • High Knees — 20m. Drive knees to hip height, stay on the balls of the feet. Cue: “Pump your arms like pistons.”
  • Butt Kicks — 20m. Heels snap toward the glutes, lean slightly forward. Cue: “Kick up, not back.”
  • Frankensteins — 20m. Walking, kick one straight leg up toward the opposite hand. Alternate legs. Cue: “Tall posture, leg goes to the hand — not hand to the leg.”
  • A-Skips — 20m. Skip with an exaggerated knee drive each step. Rhythm over speed. Cue: “Up, up, up — don’t rush it.”
  • Carioca (Karaoke) — 20m each direction. Lateral shuffle with crossover step. Stay low and loose. Cue: “Hips open, quick feet.”
  • Leg Swings (front/back) — 10 reps each leg. Hold a fence or wall. Swing freely from the hip.
  • Leg Swings (side to side) — 10 reps each leg. Same wall. Open and close across the body.
  • 2× Strides — 60m each. Easy build from jog to about 80%, then relax. Walk back between. Cue: “Smooth, not fast. Long stride, relaxed shoulders.”

4 — Baseline: 10-Minute Lap Run 15 min

A timed lap count gives each athlete a concrete number to beat later in the season. Frame it as a starting point, not a test — no one is being judged today.

  • Course setup: Athletes run laps around the baseball field. Walk the perimeter before practice and note roughly how far one lap is — this lets you convert lap counts to distance later and makes the re-test at mid-season an apples-to-apples comparison. Mark a clear start/finish point (home plate or a cone at the entry gate works well).
  • What to say before starting: “We’re going to run for exactly 10 minutes. Your goal is to count your laps — we’ll write them down and compare at the end of the season. Run at a pace where you could hold a conversation. If you’re gasping, you’re going too fast. If you need to walk, walk — just keep moving. No racing anyone else. Ready? Go.”
  • One coach starts the timer and counts from a fixed point. A second coach or parent volunteer tallies laps for each athlete with a clipboard as they pass.
  • At 10 minutes, call time. Record each athlete’s lap count immediately — athletes will forget quickly once the game starts.
  • Easy 3-minute walk together after — active recovery before Band-Aid Tag.
  • What to watch for:
    • Athletes who go out hard and fade sharply — note for pacing conversations next practice
    • Athletes who run comfortably and evenly — likely Tier 2 or 3
    • Athletes who need to walk more than run before 5 minutes — Tier 1; plan walk/run intervals

5 — Game: Band-Aid Tag 10 min

End on a high. This game keeps everyone moving, generates a lot of laughing, and sets the expectation that practice is fun.

  • Mark a boundary on the field. Pick 2 initial taggers.
  • Rules: When tagged, cover that spot with one hand and keep running. Tagged twice → go to the “hospital” (a cone at the edge), do 10 jumping jacks, then re-enter. Last person untagged wins.
  • Coach stays on the outside of the boundary, calls out anyone who stops moving, and keeps energy up.
  • Run 2 rounds if energy holds. Reset taggers each time.
  • Cue for energy: “You have to keep moving — if I see you standing still, you’re automatically going to the hospital!”

6 — Cool Down & Stretch 5 min

Teach this today — same sequence every practice. While athletes stretch, signal parents to start gathering nearby for the end-of-practice meeting.

  • Easy walk — one slow lap around the field as a group. Shake out the legs, bring the heart rate down.
  • Standing quad stretch — 20 sec each leg. Pull heel to glute, stand tall.
  • Hamstring stretch — 20 sec each leg. Step forward, hinge at the hips, flat back. Cue: “Hinge, don’t round your back.”
  • Hip flexor lunge — 20 sec each side. Low lunge, back knee down, drive hips forward.
  • Calf stretch — 20 sec each leg. Hands on fence or wall, back leg straight, heel flat.
  • Team huddle: One thing done well today. One thing to work on next time. End with a team cheer — let the athletes pick one to use all season.

📢 End-of-Practice Parent Meeting 7 min

While athletes are stretching, invite parents to gather. Athletes finish their cool-down while coaches cover these points. Keep it tight — 7 minutes max.

  • Thank you for being here. Acknowledge that showing up on day one matters — for the athletes and the program.
  • Season at a glance. Wednesdays and Fridays, 5–6 PM, through mid-late October. All updates on TeamSnap — look for the invite from Drew Swanson if you haven’t already.
  • Drop-off and pick-up. Athletes meet in the SNS gym lobby. Do not leave until you see a coach. Pick up at Village Green at 6:00 PM unless you hear otherwise via TeamSnap.
  • We need your help. Both coaches are volunteers. We will ask for snacks, drinks, a home base adult at meets, and an extra set of eyes at practice. When we ask, please say yes. Specific requests will come through TeamSnap.
  • Jerseys. If your athlete has their jersey from last season, bring it to the next practice. Everyone will receive a jersey and should wear black shorts or leggings.
  • What to bring every practice: Water bottle, layers (evenings cool off fast in August and September), and running shoes. No dress shoes, sandals, or flat-soled shoes.
  • If your athlete is hurt or can’t make it, please let us know via TeamSnap before practice. We keep attendance and we want to make sure everyone is accounted for.
  • Questions? Find us after practice or message through TeamSnap.
