New Trier Jr. High Meet

This meet sits between Loyola and NBJH in formality — a weekday afternoon on a nice track with pre-registration, contained field events, and a finish time around 7–8 PM. Very manageable with three coaches.

Where This Meet Fits

Not as casual as NBJH, not as big as Loyola. Think of it as the middle ground.

Dimension NBJH New Trier Jr. High Loyola
Day & length Weekday, ~2.5 hrs Weekday, ~3–3.5 hrs Sunday, ~7 hrs
Registration Walk-up / day-of line Pre-registered, no line Pre-registered, formal
Track Junior high Nice track Full high school track
Field events Contained Contained, organized Spread across venue
Adults needed 2 works 3 is ideal 6 minimum
The big upside: Pre-registration means no standing in line like at NBJH. Athletes are already entered, field events are in one contained area, and the whole thing wraps up at a reasonable hour. This is a very coachable meet.

Staffing

Three adults is the sweet spot here.

Three coaches makes this very manageable. Field events and running events happen concurrently. You need a point person at each location and someone who can float and redirect athletes coming off events. Two coaches can get through it but three removes the stress.

Head Coach (1)

Floats between field and track. Handles relay baton reminders, transitions, and any escalations. Not station-locked.

Field Events Lead (1)

Stays near the field event area. Field events are contained here — this is a much calmer role than at Loyola. Knows which athletes are up and gets them there on time.

Track / Running Lead (1)

Manages athletes at the track. Knows heats, keeps batons accounted for, handles check-in reminders.

Key Logistics

Post a full lineup. Print a single sheet showing every event in order with the athletes in each. Tape it to a clipboard or post it somewhere visible. Athletes can check it themselves without tracking down a coach — this alone removes a lot of noise throughout the meet.
No registration line — use that time. Unlike NBJH, there is no walk-up line when you arrive. Athletes are already entered. Use the first 20–30 minutes for a proper warm-up and full group briefing before anything starts.
Confirm your roster before you go. Pre-registration means changes after the deadline are not easy. Verify every athlete is correctly entered at the last practice before the meet.

Gear Checklist

Shorter meet, but the essentials still apply.

Batons

The venue had batons in 2026, but always bring your own — you need them for warm-up and you should never rely on borrowed equipment. Batons travel with the team, every meet.

Posted Lineup Sheet

Print every event in order with athletes listed. Tape it somewhere athletes can read it without asking a coach. High value, low effort.

Water

Weekday, after school, active kids. Bring water bottles. Even for a 3-hour meet hydration matters.

Snacks

Athletes coming straight from school may not have eaten since lunch. Granola bars or fruit for a 4:30 PM start makes a difference.

Layers

May evenings cool off once the sun goes down. Athletes will be sitting between events — an extra layer in the bag is smart.

Pre-Meet Athlete Briefing

Run this before the meet starts. Five minutes is enough.

📋
You’re already registered — know your events

Your events were submitted in advance. Check the lineup sheet, know what you’re in, and be ready when your event is called. You can’t add events the day of.

📒
Use the posted lineup

There’s a printed schedule showing every event in order. Check it when you finish something. It tells you what’s next without needing to find a coach.

⏱
Don’t miss your event

Missing check-in means you are scratched. Listen for announcements and when in doubt, check in early.

🏁
Cheer for your teammates

Between events, get near the track or field events and cheer. That energy matters.

📢 What to Communicate to Parents

  • Weekday meet, reasonable hours. Starts at 4:30 PM, wraps up around 7:00–8:00 PM. Much shorter than Loyola. Pickup time will be communicated once the schedule is clearer.
  • Pre-registered — no line. Athletes are already entered. No walk-up registration or line to deal with on arrival.
  • Coming from school. If your athlete is coming straight from school, make sure they have eaten something and have water before they arrive.
  • Missing an event = scratched. Make sure your athlete knows their events and is listening for announcements.
  • Nice venue. Well-run meet on a good track. A great way to close out the season.

Year-Over-Year Notes

Things to carry forward from 2026.

  • Post a full lineup. A single sheet showing every event in order with athletes listed is one of the highest-value things you can bring. Athletes self-direct, coaches stay focused on transitions.
  • Always bring batons. The venue had them in 2026, but bring your own anyway — for warm-up, for backup, and because the rule is batons always travel with the team.
  • Three coaches makes this smooth. Field events are contained so you don’t need Loyola-level staffing, but one adult per zone (field, track, float) keeps everything running cleanly.
  • No registration line is a real advantage. Use that time for warm-up and a proper group briefing instead of wasting it standing in line.
  • Tell parents the pickup window in advance. A 4:30 start finishing at 7–8 can catch families off guard if they don’t know to plan for it.
